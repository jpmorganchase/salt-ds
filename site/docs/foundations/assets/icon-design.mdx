Salt icons combine crisp outward-facing corners, flat line endings and softened inner corners. Simple silhouettes and open space keep them recognizable at **12px**, the smallest supported interface size.

Use this guide when designing or reviewing artwork. [Icons](/salt/foundations/assets/index) covers choosing and using icons; [Creating icons](/salt/foundations/assets/creating-icons) explains the contribution workflow.

## Meaning and family

Start with one action, object or concept. Check the catalogue before drawing: an existing icon may already communicate the intended meaning. Choose by the drawing and its established use, as some names have legacy meanings. For example, Bank check depicts a cheque, and School depicts a graduation cap.

Keep the features that distinguish the subject. A laptop with a plain screen has a broader meaning than one containing a chart. Simplify secondary detail until each remaining feature contributes to recognition. Larger brand illustrations inform proportions and character; their level of detail may be unsuitable for system icons.

<IconDesignExamples example="family" />

Related icons share visible landmarks: a cloud's lobes, a page's fold, a bookmark's notch or a person's head and shoulders. Preserve the base object's proportions, apparent size and position when adding a state or action. Compare the final drawings together; fitting them separately can make an unchanged object appear to move. For openings such as Storefront's window and door, compare the full clear shape and surrounding rim at each supported weight. Matching their centers alone is insufficient. Reuse geometry by default; a deliberate optical adjustment needs a before-and-after comparison of the complete pair at native size.

Standing figures share head size, shoulder level, leg axes and foot baseline. A combined Man–woman icon reuses the standalone figures; placing them together should not introduce boxed legs, different clothing proportions or a new scale.

Review meaning with the name hidden first, then in its intended interface. If the silhouette suggests the wrong object, correct it before refining details. When replacing established artwork, compare against the previous icon at 12px, especially for status. Keep states distinguishable without relying on color. An expert visual review is useful, but does not replace recognition feedback from users.

## Action and state modifiers

Choose the meaning before the mark. A plus, minus, slash and X communicate different actions or states. A cutout is a way to separate shapes; it does not add meaning by itself.

| Meaning                      | Usual treatment                             | Distinction to preserve                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Add or create                | Base object with a plus                     | Keep the object's identifying silhouette.                                                                                                                       |
| Remove an item or membership | Base object with a minus                    | Remove bookmark differs from an unselected or disabled bookmark.                                                                                                |
| Delete the underlying object | Established delete or trash symbol          | Deletion differs from removing an item from a collection.                                                                                                       |
| Off or unavailable           | The family's negation mark, usually a slash | Preserve familiar exceptions, such as the speaker and X for Volume off. A disabled control can keep its ordinary icon and use the component's disabled styling. |
| Clear a value or selection   | The established clear action, often an X    | Clear, close and error need distinct labels and context.                                                                                                        |
| Selected or unselected       | The product's established state treatment   | Outline and solid do not imply selection by themselves.                                                                                                         |

Preserve established meanings when extending a family. Schedule is the calendar-plus action; Schedule time is a document with a clock. Search synonyms help discovery but do not make these meanings interchangeable.

### Decide where the modifier belongs

1. Start with the complete base object and its nearest action siblings.
2. Prefer an internal mark when there is a useful empty field. Replace secondary contents, such as date dots or text lines, before compressing the mark.
3. Use an adjacent or overlapping mark when an internal mark would hide a defining feature. Keep placement and apparent scale consistent across that family's actions.
4. Add transparent clearance where shapes overlap or would visually merge. Preserve enough of the base silhouette to identify it at 12px.
5. If the result is crowded, simplify contents or change placement before reducing the mark's size or weight.

Schedule keeps Calendar's frame, header and bindings, and places the plus in the date field. Cloud upload's arrow crosses the cloud's lower edge, so it needs clearance around the arrow. Add user and Remove user keep the complete person beside a separate mark; a nearby modifier alone is no reason to cut the person.

Remove bookmark places a minus within the ribbon. The outline paints the minus; the solid reveals it as a transparent opening. This preserves the same action and placement without requiring an overlapping badge.

### Junctions within a modifier

A modifier can be separate from its base and still contain joined parts. A plus has four inner corners around its crossing; soften those while preserving flat ends and clear arms. Keep the plus separate from the calendar or page. Likewise, soften an arrow's inner shaft-to-head joins while preserving its pointed tip and straight arms.

## Canvas and optical size

Draw on a **16 × 16 canvas** and preserve natural proportions. The complete painted shape, including line endings and pointed joins, must fit inside it. Narrow icons retain space along their shorter axis; do not stretch them to fill a square.

The canvas defines the maximum available space. Choose the intended visual size from the nearest family, then judge it beside related controls and text at 12px. Fuller objects and status containers can approach the edges; compact controls retain more surrounding space. There is no mandatory inset for the whole set.

The exporter uses a **15.5-unit longest painted span at stroke width 1.5** as its default starting frame. This is not a requirement to enlarge every drawing. Record a smaller family span or an optical offset when needed. Related states and outline/solid pairs may need a shared frame to keep unchanged features aligned. See [export fitting](/salt/foundations/assets/creating-icons#fit-every-export-to-the-viewbox) for the contribution workflow.

<IconDesignExamples example="scale" />

Judge recognition at actual **12px and 16px**, then enlarge the drawing to inspect construction. Align important horizontal and vertical edges to the pixel grid where useful, checking the final size and stroke weight. Snapping every coordinate to an integer does not guarantee a sharp result.

### Align important edges to the pixel grid

Prioritize long horizontal and vertical painted edges at the default 12px size. With the default 4/3-unit primary stroke, these lines render about 1 CSS pixel wide: their centers should land on half-pixel positions so both visible edges land on pixel boundaries. For filled bars, align the visible boundaries directly. Judge the final export after scaling and fitting, not only the construction coordinates.

Keep repeated lines evenly spaced, preserve shared landmarks between variants, and move connected shapes together. Adjust the optical frame or the owning geometry when automatic fitting would undo useful alignment. An icon does not need to occupy the maximum available span if a slightly smaller frame produces a clearer drawing. Printer uses this approach for its housing and sheets.

Check the result at 16px too. A single drawing cannot put every edge exactly on the pixel grid at both sizes, stroke settings and display scales. Prefer recognition and consistent apparent weight over mechanically snapping every point. Preserve diagonal angles, natural curves and welded transitions; the chevrons are judged by their proportions and visible weight, not by a horizontal/vertical grid score.

Make **12px on a standard-density screen (device pixel ratio 1) at 100% browser zoom** the primary clarity check. Review in an actual component as well as in an icon sheet, then confirm 16px and higher-density screens. Fractional layout positions, browser zoom and display pixel density can change the raster result. Keep normal antialiasing (`shape-rendering: auto`); forcing `crispEdges` across the set can roughen diagonals and curved welds. These checks improve the artwork's starting alignment, but do not guarantee identical pixels in every context.

### Judge apparent size and balance

Equal bounds do not guarantee equal apparent size. Compare circles, diagonals and filled shapes beside related icons and text. A broad solid shape may need a smaller span; an asymmetric shape may need an optical offset. Preserve common landmarks when making these adjustments.

Close and its compact companion keep their dismissal scale; First and Last share a compact pagination frame. Directional triangles retain shallow proportions, while Key control remains a small raised keyboard glyph. A Warning container and a Play symbol are reviewed in their own families, even though both are triangular. Shape alone does not determine size.

When replacing an established icon, compare its painted width, height, proportions and placement with the previous artwork in the same display box. Use that as a recognition baseline rather than copying every old coordinate. Correct proportions in the drawing before choosing its final scale. Preserve stroke weight when changing framing, then recheck inner joins, gaps and pixel alignment. New family members should inherit these reviewed relationships.

Judge a mark within its container separately. A mathematically centered tick can look low because of its lower elbow. Balance the surrounding space and compare the positive and inverse forms. A primary status tick and a small tick qualifying another object can share a gesture without sharing one absolute size.

### Circular action families

Compare the whole arrow: circle diameter and centre, head size and placement, opening, weld strength and internal content. Matching only the outer bounds or the shaft-to-head angle does not establish a consistent family.

Refresh, Replay, Forward, History, Sync and Sparkle refresh share one circle and head construction. Preserve those proportions when adding content. History uses the Replay loop with shorter clock hands centred inside it; leave clear space around the hands before changing their weight. Undo and Redo use the same circle and mirrored heads, with their open return arcs ending at the bottom. Sync uses two opposing heads and shorter arcs. These openings distinguish actions without changing the head size.

Keep the circle centre fixed when shortening an arc. Recentering the remaining painted shape can move an otherwise unchanged head. Review internal numerals, hands and sparkles separately, then compare the complete family at 12px and 16px. New members inherit the nearest shared construction; any different framing or head proportion needs a specific visual reason.

## Stroke weight

Use the primary line weight for the silhouette and the marks that carry the icon's meaning. A lighter secondary line is appropriate only when it creates a useful hierarchy and remains clear at 12px. Keep comparable features equally weighted across the family and across outline/solid pairs.

**Solve crowding through spacing, placement or simplification before reducing an identifying mark's weight.** Thin lines can look tidy when enlarged yet disappear or feel weak at interface size. Judge the visible band, including any adjoining fill, rather than the stroke value alone.

<IconDesignExamples example="stroke" />

Dashboard's four scale ticks are separate from the rim, but remain at the same primary weight as the rim and needle. Their clearance comes from placement. In the solid variant, the tick openings match the needle opening's width. Being detached, short or inside another shape does not make a mark secondary.

<IconDesignExamples example="gauge" />

React icons use a primary width of **4/3 units in high and medium density** and **1 unit in low, touch and mobile density**. At 12px, a 4/3-unit stroke renders at approximately 1 CSS pixel. Secondary strokes retain their proportions. Standalone SVGs and CSS masks retain the artwork's **0.67-unit reference width**; they do not inherit React stroke settings. Filled marks retain their contours when surrounding strokes change. See [stroke settings](/salt/foundations/assets/index#stroke-weight).

## Geometry

**Soften exposed inner joins and interior negative-space corners. Keep outward-facing corners, points and flat terminals sharp. Preserve natural curves.** Apply this rule to the finished painted boundary, including details inside the icon.

<IconDesignExamples example="geometry" />

“Inner” describes an inward-facing (concave) corner in the finished painted boundary; it does not require an enclosed hole or a position inside the icon. A plus (+) and a diagonal cross (×) each have four concave recesses where their arms meet. Soften those joins while keeping their outward-facing corners and flat ends sharp. An outer contour can contain both kinds of corner.

An outline square has sharp outward-facing corners and softened corners around its opening. A filled square has no opening. Classify the visible boundary before choosing its treatment; the number of source paths or overlapping lines does not decide whether a corner needs softening.

| Visible relationship                            | Treatment                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Connected members forming an exposed inner join | Ease every visible inner sector where the members meet.                             |
| A corner around an interior opening             | Soften it, including angled and triangular openings.                                |
| An outward-facing corner, tip or flat terminal  | Keep it sharp.                                                                      |
| An already smooth curve or tangent transition   | Preserve it; no extra rounding is needed.                                           |
| Separate objects                                | Keep clear separation; do not weld them together.                                   |
| A join covered by a filled surface              | Assess the remaining visible boundary; do not add a line to expose the hidden join. |

### Welding and softened corners

**Welding** is the curved inner transition where connected parts meet, such as a column attaching to a rail. Softening the corner of an isolated opening follows the same visual language but is not a connection between separate parts.

Review the whole boundary, including internal supports, holes, notches and curve-to-line transitions. In Add to grid, the outline tile openings need softened corners, while the plus has its own welded crossing. The empty gaps between the tiles are not joins. In Add document, preserve the outward page-fold point while inspecting the inside fold elbow.

The larger references vary in their corner treatment. They do not establish a reliable triangle-versus-rectangle or object-role rule. Use the system standard above consistently, with a specific small-size comparison for any retained sharp interior corner.

### Calibrate the amount of welding

There is **no universal corner radius**. Compare the visible transition with the local painted band, available straight length, neighboring clearance and the nearest related construction. A compact plus, a column attachment and a curved globe intersection have different space available.

Simple marks with short arms, such as a close cross or compact arrow, often need a quieter transition to preserve their direction and proportions. Reduce the amount of softening at equivalent joins together; do not leave arbitrary joins sharp. Choose the smallest transition that remains visible across the supported weights without making the center look swollen or interrupting the straight arms. Judge it first at 12px on a standard-density screen, then at 16px and higher densities. Complexity, icon role and outline or solid styling do not define separate radius rules.

A successful transition is visible at the supported weights, leaves useful straight lengths and keeps neighboring openings clear. A curve hidden beneath a wider stroke does not produce a visible weld. Excessive softening can close a counter, turn a plus into a sparkle or consume an arrow's arms.

For compact arrows, check both arms and the point at 12px. Reduce the local radius or simplify surrounding detail before weakening the arrow. A secondary arrow can use a lighter family treatment when its role supports it; small size alone is not a reason to thin it.

On a curved arrow, make the shaft's final approach flow into the middle of the head, between its two arms. Meeting at the same endpoint is not enough: a shaft arriving at a different angle can make the head look lopsided, even when both joins are smooth. Align that approach before calibrating the weld, then compare mirrored and repeated heads together. Preserve the distant curve and neighboring marks when making a local correction.

The bend of the shaft can consume space on one side of the head. If a smaller weld still crowds the arms, adjust the head's position along the curve or its proportions while preserving a clear direction. Compare both directions and repeated heads together.

Use the same treatment for equivalent repeated geometry. First reduce local softening or remove nonessential detail if space is tight. Retain a sharp interior corner only when comparison shows that softening obscures a distinguishing feature or closes a required opening. Record the exact feature and reason with the artwork. A status role, a shape name or an old reference alone is not an exemption.

## Outline and solid variants

Both variants express the same meaning and preserve the subject's proportions, defining silhouette, direction and feature positions. Introduce fill on meaningful surfaces while retaining the lines that describe the object. A solid variant need not fill every available area.

<IconDesignExamples example="variants" />

Keep common lines equally weighted and common landmarks stable in the final exports. Filling to the middle of an outline can remove half its rim and make the object shrink. Preserve the visible boundary, or design an inset outline from the preferred solid silhouette. Either variant can lead the design.

Compare pairs side by side and by switching them in the same position. Calendar retains its bindings and header; Edit retains its complete pencil, cap and pointed nib while the barrel fills. Filling one part must not move an unchanged handle, frame or modifier. Small optical adjustments to openings are acceptable when they improve balance while preserving these relationships.

Shared status symbols use the same contour and placement in positive and inverse form within a pair. Judge their emphasis at 12px. Different containers can require different optical proportions; an exclamation mark in a triangle need not have the same dimensions as one in an octagon.

### Text lines and inverse openings

An isolated **painted text bar** has outward-facing corners and flat ends. The equivalent **transparent slot** in a solid surface has interior corners, so it can have restrained softening. This difference follows the boundary rule; it is not a separate rounded style for solid icons.

Keep line count, lengths, spacing, alignment and apparent weight consistent across the pair. Preserve a flat section at each end of a softened slot; avoid turning short text lines into pills. Check at 12px that the lines still read as the same content. These bars are independent marks, so they need no invented welding at their ends.

## Transparency and cutouts

Use true transparency so openings reveal any background. Distinguish an **inverse mark**, such as a minus cut into a filled bookmark, from **clearance around a foreground object**, such as the space between an upload arrow and a cloud.

<IconDesignExamples example="cutouts" />

Clearance follows the foreground's actual painted outline, including curves, sharp tips and flat cap corners. Measure space between visible edges, not between centerlines or bounding boxes. Curved relief around a sharp foreground corner can maintain even separation while the foreground stays crisp.

Check the complete opening at both themed stroke widths. Wider strokes consume more room while fixed cutouts stay unchanged. Rebuild clearance when the foreground contour changes; a cutout made for an unwelded arrow may no longer fit its softened roots. Preserve the mark and adjust the surrounding opening before reducing its weight.

Keep retained contours continuous. An opening reaching the outside, such as Home's doorway or Printer's paper exit, must leave no line across its mouth. Inspect where borders end beside a cutout for stray steps, slivers and seams. Preserve intentional attachments and intrinsic openings, such as handles and eyelets.

### Architectural openings

Use rectangular entrances with restrained inner-corner softening. Keep the outside of their frames sharp; easing a corner should not turn a rectangular doorway into an arch. Preserve an arch when it is a deliberate architectural feature, such as the central opening in Bank.

Generic entrance openings continue through the lower edge. Keep the ground or wall line on either side, without drawing a threshold across the opening. A deliberate step or plinth can retain its own continuous base. Keep the entrance in the same architectural position in outline and solid. Start with shared geometry, then judge the visible doorway: a painted outline frame can look wider than a transparent opening in a solid wall. A modestly narrower outline door can balance that difference while retaining the primary stroke, an open threshold and readable clear space. Keep the solid opening when it already reads well; record any local width or placement adjustment.

Compare both the clear opening and the complete visible frame after the rim is painted. An open threshold can be too narrow to read, while an oversized frame can make the same doorway look too wide. Give comparable buildings a consistent rhythm between windows and entrances. Review outline gaps separately from solid openings: two outlined frames consume space that two cutouts do not. Judge the visible boundaries at 12px and 16px rather than imposing one centerline distance on different constructions.

Give comparable window and door frames the same structural line weight. If an opening becomes crowded, adjust its dimensions and surrounding space before thinning its rim. Repeated keys and other small filled details need enough painted area and consistent pixel placement to remain evenly weighted at 12px. A display opening should retain enough height to read as a screen in both variants, rather than collapsing into a short text-like slot.

## Lettering and numerals

Use Roboto vector letter shapes to match the accompanying interface typography. Preserve natural proportions and readable counters. Use proportional lining numerals for short labels such as 10, 15 and 123; tabular spacing reserves unnecessary room around a narrow 1. Center the complete visible label, including rounded-digit overshoot, instead of centering by character count.

Start with the typeface's natural advances and the family's shared tracking. Judge the visible rhythm of the whole label; equal bounding-box gaps are not a typography rule. Keep the same numeral scale across a timer family rather than shrinking only double-digit labels. Check both inter-character spacing and clearance to the surrounding arrow at native size.

<IconDesignExamples example="lettering" />

Use the same spacing method for caption lettering: CC is one centered label, not two letters positioned independently in halves of the frame. Preserve the lettering and its spacing when switching between painted and inverse forms; a disabled slash may interrupt letters but must not move them.

Keep file labels, timer numerals and text controls consistent within their families. Bold and italic controls retain their recognizable differences. Check letters and short number sequences at 12px and 16px; do not compress them until they lose their identifying features.

## Brand logos

Preserve official logo contours and aspect ratios, and follow the owner's usage guidance. Do not apply Salt welding, substitute lettering or invent variants. Provide required clear space in the surrounding layout. Keep source artwork and provenance with the contribution.

The existing LinkedIn outline is a specific Salt presentation of the original letters without the square. Its letters align with the official solid mark; it is not an owner-supplied outline variant or a precedent for restyling other logos.

## Design review

Review the complete affected family at native **12px and 16px**, on **light and dark backgrounds**, at the supported stroke weights. Use an enlarged view to diagnose geometry, then return to native size. Include the intended component context and compare both React icons and baked SVG or CSS-mask artwork when contributing changes.

Before accepting a design, check:

- **Meaning:** the object, action, direction and state remain recognizable, including without color.
- **Family:** apparent size, weight, landmarks and modifier placement agree with related icons.
- **Visible weight:** inspect the narrowest painted bands and inverse openings, including rims made from filled shapes. Equal stroke settings do not guarantee equal visible thickness.
- **Weld strength:** joins remain softened without swallowing straight arms or closing counters. Judge this separately from line weight and pixel clarity.
- **Boundaries:** every exposed inner join and opening follows the corner rule; outer points and flat ends stay clear.
- **Pairs:** filling preserves the subject, common features and positive/inverse marks.
- **Space:** cutouts follow the foreground; counters stay open; no seams, fragments or clipped tips appear.
- **Detail:** text, numerals and small marks remain useful at 12px.

Record deliberate exceptions and unresolved decisions with the artwork. Automated checks can find construction defects, but do not establish recognition or complete visual approval. [Creating icons](/salt/foundations/assets/creating-icons#use-the-existing-review-surfaces) explains how to prepare the comparison and contribution.
