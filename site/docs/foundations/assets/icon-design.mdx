Salt icons use simple shapes, open space and restrained detail to communicate a single action, object or concept. Consistent proportions and construction make related icons recognizable as a family.

Use this guidance to understand the style of Salt icons and judge their visual consistency. Browse [Icons](/salt/foundations/assets/index) to find a symbol and learn how to use it in an interface. For artwork creation and maintenance instructions, see [Creating icons](/salt/foundations/assets/creating-icons).

## Meaning and family

Start with the meaning an icon needs to communicate and the context in which it appears. Compare the actual drawings in the catalogue, including closely related objects, actions and states. An existing icon may already express the intended meaning.

Keep the features that distinguish the subject. A generic laptop has a plain screen; adding a chart would suggest a more specific use. Similarly, a sun and a brightness control can share a visual language without every detail being interchangeable. Check an icon's established meaning before using its name as a design brief: `bank-check` represents a cheque, `scales` a weighing scale, and `school` a graduation cap.

<IconDesignExamples example="family" />

Related icons should share visible landmarks: the contour of a cloud, the proportions of a page, the geometry of a person's head and shoulders, or the positions of a panel's rails. Reuse those relationships when adding a state or action. Leave deliberate space for an action mark and preserve its direction without distorting the base object.

Simplify detail until each remaining feature helps identify the subject or distinguish it from its neighbors. Check meaning in the intended interface as well as in isolation.

## Canvas and optical size

Salt icons use a **16 × 16 canvas**. The entire visible shape must fit inside that frame, including the outer tips of joins and line endings.

The generator normally scales and centers each icon export so its longest painted dimension targets **15.5 units at a primary stroke width of 1.5**, leaving **0.25 units** at each end of that axis. Reviewed optical exceptions may use a smaller span or an offset center while remaining inside the canvas. It preserves the drawing's aspect ratio: a narrow arrow or a broad calendar retains space along its shorter axis. This shared framing applies to both outline and solid exports, which are fitted separately.

Author recognizable geometry and useful internal spacing in the owning recipe; the generator supplies the final framing. Do not add recipe-specific padding or stretch a drawing to fill both axes. Record a justified framing adjustment in the optical-fit configuration described in [Creating icons](/salt/foundations/assets/creating-icons#fit-every-export-to-the-viewbox). Thinner strokes occupy slightly less of the frame, especially at acute joins. Official brand marks retain their existing 16-unit framing. The full-canvas solid checkmark and its solid aliases also retain their 16-unit control surface.

<IconDesignExamples example="scale" />

Judge icons at their intended interface size. Salt's default icon size follows the interface density and has a **12px minimum**. See [Scaling icons](/salt/foundations/assets/index#scaling-icons) for the density scale.

Use enlarged views to inspect geometry, then return to native **12px and 16px** views to judge recognition, balance and useful detail.

### Judge apparent size and balance

Equal painted bounds are a starting point, not a guarantee of equal apparent size. Compare circles, broad filled shapes and diagonals beside related icons and text. A filled shape can need a slightly smaller span than its outline; a play triangle can need a small shift toward its point to balance its heavier flat side. Preserve natural proportions and use local comparisons rather than assigning one correction to every shape.

Judge an interior mark separately from its container. A tick can sit low inside a mathematically centered circle because of its lower elbow. Move the mark within the shared container when that improves the surrounding space; do not center asymmetric ink by its centroid alone. Compare positive marks and transparent counters together.

## Brand logos

Brand logos are an exception to Salt's icon drawing style. Use the brand owner's current official artwork, preserving its supplied contours and aspect ratio. Do not restyle a logo to match Salt geometry or stroke weight, add an outline, or invent variants. Follow the owner's usage guidance.

For GitHub, use the current Invertocat from the [GitHub logo guidance](https://brand.github.com/foundations/logo). Use black or white by default, with sufficient contrast; grey and green are limited uses governed by GitHub's guidance. Keep clear space of at least **25% of the logo's width** around the mark. Provide that space in the surrounding layout; it is not mandatory padding inside the SVG viewBox.

## Stroke weight

Generated React icons use the theme's primary width of **1.5 units in high and medium densities**, and **1 unit in low, touch and mobile densities** on the 16-unit canvas. The [size foundation token](/salt/foundations/size#stroke-width) `--salt-size-icon-strokeWidth` adjusts the weight across an application or subtree; `--saltIcon-strokeWidth` overrides it for an individual icon. If neither variable is defined, the component falls back to 1. Stroke and geometry scale together: `W × P / 16` gives the rendered primary width in pixels. High and medium density icons use 1.5 units at 12px, producing a **1.125px** primary stroke.

Numeric SVG masters retain a **0.67-unit reference width**, which renders at 0.5025px at 12px. Standalone SVG images and CSS masks use the baked widths and do not inherit the host's stroke variables. Filled contours and official brand logos keep their supplied shapes.

<IconDesignExamples example="stroke" />

Secondary details can use a lighter line to avoid competing with the main shape. The hands in Schedule time, for example, have less weight than the surrounding clock. Preserve those ratios when the primary width changes, and judge the resulting balance at interface size.

At small sizes, crowded intersections can look heavier than isolated lines of the same width. Simplify redundant detail, open counters or reduce the weight of secondary lines locally before changing the whole set. Keep the silhouette and the features that carry meaning. A detail that is attractive enlarged still needs to contribute at 12px.

## Geometry

Build recognizable forms from straight runs, deliberate angles, broad curves and open shapes. Keep crisp exterior corners where they define the silhouette. Introduce small curved junctions where the object or the connection between features calls for them.

Salt's standard line endings are **flat** and stop at their endpoints. Keep sharp joins where they define the shape. Inspect acute corners and arrowheads closely, since their tips can crowd nearby details or extend beyond the canvas.

<IconDesignExamples example="geometry" />

Use curved junctions deliberately. Bank columns meet the roof and plinth with eased attachments, the calendar has locally eased junctions, and the globe's curves meet as parts of one sphere. Keep these transitions consistent with the object's shape and the rhythm of its repeated features.

There is no universal corner radius for the family. A softly curved housing and a sharp structural corner can belong to the same icon. Follow the object and the nearest related construction, then check the result at native size.

## Outline and solid variants

Outline and solid variants express the same meaning through different use of filled surfaces. Preserve their proportions, dividers, action positions and directional relationships in the shared recipe. The generator fits each variant to its own painted bounds, so exported scale and placement can differ while the underlying features remain consistent.

<IconDesignExamples example="variants" />

Choose a surface that can fill while keeping the icon's defining features clear. The solid calendar fills its date field and retains an open header. In `panel-open-left`, the rail fills while the frame and arrow remain shared line features. A file-format icon can fill its folded corner while keeping the page and label open.

Keep shared line features equally weighted in both variants. Export fitting preserves the authored stroke widths and their lighter secondary ratios, even when an outline and its solid variant need different geometry scales. Filled surfaces provide the difference in emphasis. Inspect the final fitted pair as well as the recipes: independent fitting can move internal landmarks. Derive positive and inverse marks from the same gesture where possible, then judge their weight and placement on their different backgrounds.

Use transparent counters where a filled surface needs an opening. Small filled details can also appear in an outline variant; the distinction is the treatment of meaningful surfaces. Add a solid companion when it offers a coherent treatment, and compare the pair at the same size and weight.

## Lettering and numerals

Salt's letters and numerals use the family's Open Sans letter shapes. Preserve their natural proportions and spacing. Avoid stretching letters to equal widths or simplifying them so far that their characteristic shapes and counters disappear.

<IconDesignExamples example="lettering" />

Keep regular lettering restrained in weight. The bold control uses a heavier filled letter, and the italic control uses the family's barred I. Preserve these recognizable differences while balancing them with neighboring controls.

Center the visible contours, not the number of characters. Rounded digits can extend slightly beyond a flat alignment line to appear the same height; retain that optical adjustment when centering numerals. Forward and replay timers share their circle geometry and center the actual numeral shapes. File-format labels share a page shape and an external baseline.

Keep letter proportions consistent throughout the family. Check counters, letter spacing and short numeric sequences at 12px and 16px.

## Transparency and cutouts

An action mark or overlapping object must remain distinguishable from the shape behind it. The open space around it should follow the foreground's actual contour. Openings must work on light and dark backgrounds and reveal the surface behind the icon.

<IconDesignExamples example="cutouts" />

Use true transparency for openings. A patch colored to match one background will become visible when the background changes.

Judge the gap between **visible edges** at the configured stroke width. Wider strokes occupy more space while filled cutout contours remain fixed. Keep the separation visually even around straight sides, curves, line endings and corners. An enlarged outline around an object's bounding box can still leave uneven space around the shape itself.

The devices cutout follows the phone's curved corner. The tag-clear cutout buffers the full painted X while preserving its flat terminals. In cloud-sync, overlapping cutouts remove unusable slivers without changing the action's meaning.

Keep intentional attachments and intrinsic openings intact. A connected handle, a letter counter and a tag eyelet each have their own purpose; a small opening is not automatically a separation defect.

## Design review

Compare the icons beside their closest family members in the [catalogue](/salt/foundations/assets/index#all-icons) and in their intended interface. Check both recognition and consistency with the surrounding controls.

Inspect each variant at actual **12px and 16px** on **light and dark** backgrounds. Start with the density's themed width: **1.5 for high/medium** and **1 for low/touch/mobile**. Compare the **0.67** reference when reviewing its SVG master. Use the existing Storybook **Icons/Icon** catalogue and search stories, **Icon QA** size and CSS-mask stories, and relevant component examples. Select density and light/dark mode through the Storybook toolbar. For exact sizes, stroke overrides and temporary view-box outlines, follow [Creating icons](/salt/foundations/assets/creating-icons#use-the-existing-review-surfaces). Check final gaps and clipping at every chosen width; the framing target reserves space through width 1.5.

Include an enlarged view for contours, joins, overlaps and cutouts, then return to actual size to judge readability.

Before accepting a design, check that:

- Its meaning, direction and state are clear in context.
- Its apparent size, visual weight and feature positions fit related icons.
- Outline and solid variants retain their recipe features and line weights, with each export fitted to its own painted bounds.
- Lettering has readable counters and spacing.
- Cutouts follow the foreground and reveal the background.
- Each ordinary export meets the default framing or its documented optical target at width 1.5, preserves its aspect ratio and stays inside the 16-unit frame.
- There are no unintended mergers, fragments, clipped tips or collapsed openings.
- Details remain useful at native size, and enlarged inspection reveals no malformed geometry.

Judge a narrow detail by whether it remains useful in context. A larger, cleaner-looking shape is only an improvement if it preserves the icon's meaning and balance with its family.
